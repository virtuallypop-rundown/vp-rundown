Tuesday 17 November 2026

A bigger artist development intake and a youth music funding deadline lead today, alongside new figures on grassroots venue finances and an expanded voice-and-likeness case against Suno.

Needs attention

Artist development

iluvlive doubles its development cohort to four artists

Live music platform iluvlive's Artist Development Programme, run with publisher Stellar Songs, is open until 5pm on Friday 27 November 2026. Listings say this round will take four artists instead of the usual two. Each artist gets £5,000 for costs such as mixing, mastering, PR, plugging, videos and tour support, plus 12 months of mentoring, A&R input, DSP pitching and help with release plans. The programme's FAQ says it is open to England-based artists because of its Arts Council funding.

Why it matters: This is one of the few UK schemes that combines cash with hands-on release and A&R support, and its alumni include acts that went on to sign deals. With more places this round, it's worth putting forward any developing act you work with, and checking the eligibility notes on the application form first.

Sources: iluvlive application form · Launchpad listing

Artist development

Youth Music Trailblazer Fund closes this Friday

Round 14 of Youth Music's Trailblazer Fund, supported by Arts Council England, closes at 5pm on Friday 20 November 2026. It offers £2,000 to £30,000 to organisations in England running music projects for people aged 25 and under, for projects starting between April and June 2027. Decisions are due on 19 March 2027.

Why it matters: This one is for organisations, not individual artists. Promoters, producers and venues running workshops, youth showcases or development nights for young musicians could use it to fund that work next spring.

Sources: Youth Music

Updates

Live music & touring

Music Venue Trust sets out how Liveline rescues at-risk shows

Music Venue Trust chief executive Mark Davyd has described how its Liveline programme covers the financial risk on shows that otherwise wouldn't happen: for example, when a venue can't guarantee a fee, a promoter faces a shortfall, or a tour date doesn't add up. Figures discussed in the piece put the average grassroots venue's profit margin at 2.5%, with more than half making no profit, and say 175 UK towns and cities have lost regular professional touring.

Why it matters: These figures help explain why routing mid-sized tours outside the big cities is getting harder. Liveline is a route promoters and touring productions can look into when a show is viable in principle but too risky to book.

Sources: The InterSpace Daily · Music Venue Trust: The Evolution of the Grassroots Levy

Entertainment law

Elvis rights holders expand their claims against Suno

A week after their first complaint, Elvis Presley Enterprises, Authentic Brands Group and Raleigh Music Publishing have filed an amended lawsuit against Suno with many more examples of AI outputs they say imitate Presley's songs and voice. The claims still cover 859 works and rely partly on Tennessee's ELVIS Act and the common-law right of publicity, which protect a performer's voice and likeness. Suno has not yet answered in court, and none of the allegations has been tested.

Why it matters: This is one of the first big tests of a law that specifically protects a performer's voice from AI imitation. Whatever the outcome, it is likely to shape how voice and likeness rights are discussed across the industry, including around tribute and heritage productions.

Sources: Digital Music News · The AI Musicpreneur lawsuit tracker

Music marketing & social

Symphonic licenses its catalogue to Roblox, including short clips

Distributor Symphonic has licensed hundreds of thousands of tracks to Roblox's Creator Store, the music library developers use in their games. The deal also covers Moments, Roblox's new feature for sharing 30-second game clips. Symphonic follows other distributors, including DistroKid, in striking music deals with the platform.

Why it matters: Games are becoming another discovery channel for independent releases, especially with younger audiences. If you distribute through Symphonic, check whether your releases are opted in and what the terms are.

Sources: Music Ally

Artist development

MusiCares survey: only 30% can live comfortably from music

US charity MusiCares has published early findings from its 2026 Wellness in Music Survey of more than 3,300 music-industry professionals. Only 30% said they can comfortably cover their expenses from their music work, and the survey also raises concerns about healthcare and wellbeing.

Why it matters: The data is from the US, but it points the same way as UK evidence on artist incomes. It's useful context when planning realistic budgets, fees and support for the artists and crews you develop.

Sources: Music Ally
