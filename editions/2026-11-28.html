Saturday 28 November 2026

A quiet weekend start, with the Dutch rights body BumaStemra taking an AI background music provider to court and a US city suing Vivid Seats over how it shows ticket prices.

Updates

Entertainment law

BumaStemra takes AI music firm YourNeuroTune to court

BumaStemra, the Dutch collecting society, has confirmed it's taking legal action against YourNeuroTune, which supplies royalty-free background music to gyms and shops and is alleged to have made that music with Suno. Summary proceedings are listed for 19 February in The Hague, with IP lawyer Dirk Visser on BumaStemra's legal team.

Why it matters: This is an early test of whether AI-made background music can be sold as a way for venues and businesses to stop paying performing rights licences. A ruling could affect how collecting societies, including PRS and PPL, respond to similar services.

Sources: Digital Music News

Wider context

Live music & touring

Baltimore sues Vivid Seats over ticket pricing

The City of Baltimore has sued resale platform Vivid Seats. It alleges the platform advertised low prices that weren't really available, raised prices during checkout and added mandatory fees that only appeared at the end. Vivid Seats is also part of the resale industry group challenging Washington DC's new resale law.

Why it matters: Drip pricing and hidden fees are a target for regulators on both sides of the Atlantic, so how ticket prices are displayed is getting closer attention from authorities.

Sources: IQ Magazine
