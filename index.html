Updates

Entertainment law

Eminem's publisher adds 108 examples to Suno lawsuit

Eight Mile Style and Martin Affiliated filed an amended complaint against Suno on 19 November, less than two weeks after first suing. It adds 108 further examples of user-generated Suno tracks that the publishers say reuse lyrics or musical elements from 26 Eminem compositions, many allegedly imitating his voice, and disputes Suno's public statements about its restrictions. The 269 works, the claims and the damages sought are unchanged, Suno has not yet answered in court, and none of the allegations has been tested.

Why it matters: This is the latest of several rights holders, after Elvis Presley Enterprises and Reservoir, to strengthen claims against Suno by filing specific outputs. How the courts treat output evidence alongside training claims will shape how AI music tools handle recognisable songs and voices, an issue close to home for anyone working with well-known catalogues.

Sources: Digital Music News · Amended complaint (CourtListener)

Artist development

Study finds four in five UK live bookings led by men

Researchers at the University of Portsmouth analysed 510 live music bookings across 15 UK cities over three months and found 410 featured male lead performers, compared with 100 featuring women. The findings come from two linked pieces of analysis on gender bias in the UK live sector.

Why it matters: It gives promoters, venues and managers hard numbers to check their own bookings against, and supports the case for programmes such as PRS Foundation's Tempo Shift, which closes on 14 December.

Sources: Music Ally

Wider context

Live music & touring

Sport gets a touting ban framework before music does

The Sporting Events Bill is awaiting Royal Assent after the House of Lords agreed all the Commons amendments on 18 November. It lets ministers apply a criminal ticket touting offence, plus advertising and trading rules, to qualifying sporting events by regulations. During the Commons debate, MPs said this creates a two-tier system while music and other cultural events wait, and the minister said the separate ticket tout ban bill will deal with industrial-scale touting.

Why it matters: Concert and theatre resale remains covered only by existing consumer law until the promised ticket tout ban bill, which still hasn't been published in draft. Keep pointing fans towards authorised and capped resale in the meantime.

Sources: LexisNexis · Hansard

Music marketing & social

Ad industry body pushes for one definition of a view

The US Interactive Advertising Bureau wants platforms to agree a common definition of a view, along with shared measures of impressions, reach and watch time. Two-thirds of the 53 industry leaders it surveyed named this the top priority, and it hopes to secure one or two platform commitments in the first year, though no platform has signed up yet.

Why it matters: Following YouTube's recent changes to how it counts views, it's a reminder to report campaigns on watch time, completion and follows rather than headline view counts.

Sources: Music Ally · IAB report
