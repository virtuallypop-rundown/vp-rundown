Artist development

Free Help Musicians mentoring: apply by 23 November

Help Musicians is recruiting mentees for Soundboard, its free online mentoring network, until 5pm on Monday 23 November. Mentees get eight hours of one-to-one mentoring over six months, plus peer networking, expert-led career sessions and digital resources, with around 35 pairs planned for this intake. Applicants must be UK-based, aged 18 or over, and have no more than £10,000 in savings. They also need to earn at least 40% of their income from music, or be under three years into their career with at least £500 of music earnings in the last 12 months.

Why it matters: This is a no-cost way for a developing artist to get structured advice on branding, releases, income and audience growth. Mentees shape the agenda themselves. If you work with acts who aren't yet ready for a manager, it's worth flagging to them this week.

Sources: Help Musicians

Live music & touring

A year after the tout ban pledge, O2 finds big resale mark-ups

Virgin Media O2 has published an analysis of resale listings for Birmingham shows, a year after the government announced plans to ban reselling tickets for profit. It found average mark-ups of more than 100% for several acts and some tickets listed above £11,000. The worst case was more than 22,000% above face value at a 1,500-capacity venue. O2 and FanFair Alliance are urging ministers to deliver the promised ban and a resale price cap. The draft Ticket Tout Ban Bill still hasn't been published.

Why it matters: Touring productions playing mid-sized theatres and arenas are exposed too: fans who pay inflated prices often blame the act or the venue. Until the law changes, it pays to keep reminding fans to use authorised sellers and capped resale platforms.

Sources: Virgin Media O2

Entertainment law

The Mavericks name dispute goes to court

Betty Malo, widow of The Mavericks' late frontman Raul Malo, has sued the band's remaining members in a Tennessee court. She says the band's operating agreement stopped them using the name after his death without her consent. The suit says the agreement was amended in April without her agreement, and that a trademark application for the name followed, which she has opposed. The band members say the amendment was made before their current tour, and have said publicly that they are saddened by the case. None of the claims has been decided.

Why it matters: This is a reminder of how much depends on what partnership, LLC and trademark paperwork says about a band name when line-ups change. The question matters to any long-running production or act that tours under a continuing brand.

Sources: The Tennessean · Billboard

Music marketing & social

YouTube view counts now include people watching together on TV

YouTube is rolling out a change that adds co-views, its estimate of how many people are watching together on a TV, to public view counts. It says viewers now watch more than a billion hours of YouTube on TVs every day. This follows an earlier change that counts a view as soon as any video starts playing. YouTube hasn't said whether the change affects analytics or monetisation.

Why it matters: Public view counts on music videos and live clips may rise without any change in reach. Be careful comparing new figures with older ones in reports to clients, promoters or venues, and rely on your own analytics for decisions.

Sources: Music Ally
