Friday 27 November 2026

A lighter Friday: Gig Lab wins Innovate UK funding to trial AI booking tools for small venues and promoters, Sound and Music opens a new round of small grants for music creators, and Tidal confirms its ban on fully AI-generated tracks starts on 9 December.

Needs attention

Artist development

Sound and Music Essentials Fund registration closes 6 December

Round Five of Sound and Music's Essentials Fund offers grants of £250 to £450 for things like courses, mentoring, software, hardware, recording, collaborator fees or venue hire linked to a creator's own composing practice. Registration closes at 23:59 GMT on 6 December. Fifty registrants are then drawn at random, with half the places kept for applicants from under-represented groups, and invited to apply in full between 14 December and 10 January. Applicants must be 18 or over, have lived in the UK for at least two years, not be in education and not have had an Essentials grant before.

Why it matters: It's small, quick funding that suits early-career writers and producers who need one key piece of kit or training. Registering only takes a short form, though the random draw means there's no guarantee of reaching the full application stage.

Sources: Sound and Music opportunities · Neon Music

Music marketing & social

Tidal's ban on fully AI tracks starts 9 December

Following its announcement on Tuesday, Tidal has said it will start rejecting and removing wholly AI-generated tracks on 9 December. Distributors won't be able to deliver them, and Tidal expects to remove more than 29 million songs by the end of the year. It says about 27 million tracks, or 6.3% of its catalogue, are currently tagged as wholly AI-generated, and that it has already removed more than 65,000 tracks over suspected impersonation of artist names. Music made with the help of AI tools is still allowed.

Why it matters: With a firm date set, artists and labels using AI in production should make sure their distributor metadata describes the work accurately. Detection partners have acknowledged that false positives are a risk.

Sources: AI Affairs · The Irish Times

Live music & touring

Gig Lab gets funding for venue booking AI trial

Sunderland-based Gig Lab has won Innovate UK funding, reported as £75,000, through the Next Wave: Breakthrough Wave 1 competition, which drew more than 700 applications. With Northumbria University and technology partner Sauce, it will spend 12 months in 2027 building and trialling AI tools to help small venues and independent promoters judge where shows are likely to work and find venues with free dates. The company says booking decisions will stay with people. Gig Lab is already used across the Propaganda Independent Venues group, and venues and promoters can register interest in testing it.

Why it matters: Better data on where shows are likely to sell could make it less risky to add smaller towns to a tour, which matters for productions routing theatres and mid-size rooms. Promoters who want a say in how the tools work can sign up for the trial.

Sources: Music Week · Record of the Day · Dealroom
