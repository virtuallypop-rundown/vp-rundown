Weekend edition

Saturday 21 November 2026

The UK-EU summit's final word on touring, a new Instagram tool against reposted and AI-altered Reels, and a US ruling keeping a Reels music licensing case against Meta alive lead this weekend's edition.

Updates

Live music & touring

UK-EU summit commits only to further touring talks

The joint statement from Friday's Brussels summit reaffirms the value of touring artists and says both sides should explore further mutually beneficial solutions, but sets out no specific changes. UK Music welcomed the commitment to talks and urged formal negotiations soon, while the Musicians' Union said members would be disappointed that no concrete changes were made.

Why it matters: Visa, work permit, carnet and haulage rules for EU dates stay as they are for now, so 2027 European routing should still be budgeted on current costs.

Sources: GOV.UK joint statement · Arts Professional · Musicians' Union

Music marketing & social

Instagram adds protection against reposted Reels

Instagram is rolling out content protection, a tool already on Facebook, which alerts enrolled accounts when their original Reels are reposted by others, now including AI-modified copies. Owners can block the copy on Instagram and Facebook, add an 'original by' label linking back, or leave it up and track it.

Why it matters: Live clips and promo videos for touring shows are often reposted without credit, so enrolling your main accounts could help keep the views, and the audience, pointing back to you.

Sources: Music Ally

Wider context

Entertainment law

Wixen's Reels music claims against Meta move forward

A US federal judge has trimmed but largely kept alive Wixen Music Publishing's lawsuit alleging Meta kept using songs in Reels after a licence expired. Claims over pre-1978 works were dismissed because their copyright owners are not co-plaintiffs, while the remaining infringement, trade libel and defamation claims continue; none has been decided.

Why it matters: The case tests how far music available in social platforms' libraries is actually licensed, which matters to anyone building campaigns around Reels audio.

Sources: Digital Music News · Bloomberg Law
