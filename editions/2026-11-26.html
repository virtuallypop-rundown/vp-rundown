Thursday 26 November 2026

More artists join the likeness-rights class action against Suno as a judge refuses an earlier dismissal bid, a new booking tool lets pubs report live music to PPL PRS, the North gets £2.2m for a joint creative partnership, and PRS for Music agrees a licence with Roblox.

Updates

Entertainment law

More artists join likeness claim against Suno

On 24 November, Jason Isbell and David Lowery's proposed class action against Suno was amended to add around 20 artist plaintiffs, including The Chicks and the estates of Willie Colón and Sam Moore. The case argues that Suno's AI tool exploits performers' names, voices and identities without consent, under state name, image and likeness laws. A day later, a US federal judge in Boston denied Suno's earlier motion to dismiss, reportedly because the complaint had been amended. That's a procedural step, not a ruling on the merits, and Suno can still challenge the new complaint.

Why it matters: Unlike the copyright suits against Suno, this case is about identity and likeness rights. The argument that a recording licence isn't the same as permission to use a performer's persona is relevant to anyone whose business depends on an artist's sound, name or image.

Sources: Digital Music News · The Northern Current

Live music & touring

Pub gig bookings can now feed PPL PRS reports

GigRealm, a booking platform that connects artists with grassroots venues, promoters and pub operators, has partnered with PPL PRS. Venues holding TheMusicLicence can now book and pay acts in one place and send music usage declarations for those gigs from the booking record. Background music stays under the venues' existing licensing arrangements. Venues with TheMusicLicence are reportedly offered six months' free use of the platform.

Why it matters: Pubs and hospitality venues are a big part of the circuit for tribute and covers acts. Easier reporting could mean fewer licensing headaches for venues and more accurate data on live performances.

Sources: Music Week · Record of the Day

Music marketing & social

PRS for Music agrees licence with Roblox

PRS for Music has signed a licensing agreement covering music used across Roblox, the gaming and creation platform, which reported 138 million daily active users in the third quarter of 2026. PRS says royalty payments to songwriters, composers and publishers will start next year.

Why it matters: Games and virtual worlds are becoming a real space for music discovery and promotion. This deal means writers whose songs are used there should start to see income from it.

Sources: Record of the Day · Music Ally

Wider context

Artist development

North gets £2.2m to grow creative careers locally

The Culture Secretary has committed £2.2 million to The Great North Creative, a partnership of the North of England's mayors, including Liverpool City Region, with Arts Council England, the public service broadcasters and the Creative Industries Policy and Evidence Centre. The money will pay for a small team and joint projects, including trade missions, an annual showcase to attract investment and an online hub to help creatives find support. It builds on the Northern Music Export Office launched in June.

Why it matters: It's aimed at helping musicians and creative businesses in Merseyside and across the North build careers without moving to London. No application window has been announced yet, so it's worth watching for the hub and showcase details.

Sources: West Yorkshire Combined Authority · GOV.UK
