Weekday briefing

Tuesday 24 November 2026

A US appeals court rules that Mary J. Blige's 'Real Love' did not infringe a much-sampled funk track, regulators press Ticketmaster in their ticket-limits case, SXSW London opens its 2027 music applications, and Impala sets out what it wants done about streaming fraud.

Needs attention

Artist development

SXSW London 2027 music applications close 15 December

SXSW London is taking music submissions for its 2027 event, which runs from 24 to 29 May. Artists can apply to play, promoters, labels and collectives can apply to curate a showcase, and Shoreditch venues can apply to host one. All three strands close on 15 December.

Why it matters: It's a showcase in front of international industry and media without leaving the UK. The curate strand also suits promoters who want to put their own acts in front of buyers. Artists who are invited abroad can look at PRS Foundation's International Showcase Fund for support.

Sources: SXSW London · PRS for Music festival guide

Updates

Entertainment law

Appeals court rules 'Real Love' did not infringe funk classic

On 23 November, the US Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit upheld the dismissal of TufAmerica's (Tuff City Records') claim against Universal Music Publishing over Mary J. Blige's 1992 hit 'Real Love'. The claim said the song took from the composition of the Honey Drippers' 1973 track 'Impeach the President'. The three-judge panel found the songs not substantially similar as a matter of law, pointing to differences in lyrics, melody, genre, subject and overall feel despite some shared percussive elements. Tuff City's lawyer said it will seek further review.

Why it matters: The ruling concerns the composition, not the recording, and shows how US courts weigh a whole song against a borrowed drum pattern. It's useful context for producers who use samples or recreate classic grooves, and it isn't a licence to skip sample clearance.

Sources: Rolling Stone via Yahoo · Second Circuit opinion (Laws.com)

Live music & touring

FTC asks court to strike most Ticketmaster defences

The US Federal Trade Commission and seven states have filed a motion asking a federal court in California to strike 10 of the 12 defences Live Nation and Ticketmaster raised in the case over ticket purchase limits and resale. The case alleges the companies let brokers who got around ticket limits resell on Ticketmaster while it collected fees. Several of the challenged defences place responsibility on resellers and bot operators. The court hasn't ruled, and a hearing is set for 7 January 2027.

Why it matters: Together with the UK's CMA action on resale fees and the planned resale price cap, it shows regulators targeting platforms, not just touts. Promoters setting ticket limits should expect closer scrutiny of how those limits are enforced.

Sources: Digital Music News · MLQ

Wider context

Music marketing & social

Impala sets out its asks on streaming fraud

Helen Smith, executive chair of the European indie body Impala, has called for coordinated action on streaming fraud. Her asks include tougher artist verification and know-your-customer checks, penalties for repeat offenders, and distributors checking clients and content before delivery. She also wants clear routes of appeal for legitimate artists whose music is wrongly flagged or removed.

Why it matters: Impala's own guidance advises against any promotion service that promises guaranteed streams, listeners or playlist placements. Paid plays can get releases flagged or royalties held back, so check how any campaign partner actually works.

Sources: Record of the Day · Impala streaming manipulation guidance
