Friday 20 November 2026

Two paid artist development calls closing on 30 November lead today, alongside the UK-EU summit's first formal words on touring, provisional findings against viagogo over hidden fees, and TikTok's latest claims about its chart influence.

Needs attention

Artist development

Two paid development schemes close on 30 November

City Music Foundation is taking applications for its 2026/27 CMF Artist Programme until midday on Monday 30 November. It's open to UK-resident early-career soloists and ensembles who have finished full-time education and don't have commercial management or a booking agent, and successful artists can receive up to £10,000 towards a specific career project, plus mentoring and performances. An application fee applies, and shortlisted acts audition in London in late January. Separately, The Nest Collective is recruiting six England-based folk musicians for Common Ground, a new paid programme running to 2028. The panel will pair them into three new duos for rehearsal, performances, recording and industry support. Applications close on 30 November.

Why it matters: Both schemes combine money with structured support, which is what most developing acts lack. The CMF scheme suits classical, jazz and acoustic performers without representation, and Common Ground suits folk-rooted players open to collaboration. Check the eligibility rules carefully before encouraging anyone to apply.

Sources: City Music Foundation · The Nest Collective

Live music & touring

Touring artists make the UK-EU summit text

UK and EU leaders meet in Brussels today for the second UK-EU summit, where they are expected to sign deals on food trade, carbon pricing and a youth mobility scheme. Reports on the draft joint statement say it includes a commitment for both sides to explore further solutions for touring artists. That's a modest step rather than a deal, but the EU has long resisted reopening the issue. The wording follows a letter from more than 300 UK and EU cultural organisations asking for a structured dialogue on moving touring crews and equipment.

Why it matters: Any easing of visas, work permits, carnets or cabotage rules would directly cut the cost of taking a production into Europe. For now nothing changes in practice, so budget EU dates on current rules and watch for the final summit statement.

Sources: The i Paper · Yahoo News · Music Week

Entertainment law

CMA issues provisional findings on viagogo fees

The Competition and Markets Authority has issued provisional infringement notices to two companies operating three viagogo websites. It provisionally considers that UK buyers of concert and sports tickets may not have been shown mandatory fees, such as booking fees, in the first price displayed, a practice known as drip pricing. The findings are provisional, and viagogo says the investigation is ongoing and no final findings have been made. The companies can now respond, and the CMA expects to update in spring 2027. If it issues a final notice, penalties can reach 10% of worldwide turnover.

Why it matters: This is the first case under the CMA's new direct consumer enforcement powers to reach this stage without a settlement. It signals that all-in pricing from the first screen is now the expected standard for anyone selling tickets in the UK, including promoters running their own box offices.

Sources: BBC News · TicketNews

Music marketing & social

TikTok says 94% of new global hits went viral there first

TikTok has published its annual Music Impact Report, again positioning itself as a driver of streams and chart success beyond its own app. Its headline claim is that 94% of songs entering the Billboard Global 200 in 2025 went viral on TikTok first, up from 84% the year before. Music Ally notes that some of this reflects labels running pre-release TikTok campaigns for songs that might have charted anyway.

Why it matters: Treat the figure as a sign of how standard pre-release TikTok activity has become, not as proof that the platform alone creates hits. Seeding clips before a release or tour announcement is now the norm, so plan content early.

Sources: Music Ally

Wider context

Entertainment law

US music groups back Washington DC's resale price cap in court

Ten US music organisations have filed amicus briefs supporting Washington DC's RESALE Act, which caps ticket resale prices and fees and requires price transparency. Resale platforms and brokers are challenging the law in federal court.

Why it matters: The case is a useful comparison while the UK waits for its own draft legislation to cap resale prices.

Sources: Interspace Music
