Weekday briefing

Monday 16 November 2026

Two funding windows for artists and their teams lead today, alongside new figures on the £1 arena ticket levy and a possible wider reach for the UK's under-16 social media ban.

Needs attention

Artist development

Help Musicians Fast Track rounds now close 23 November

Help Musicians' official pages now give 5pm on Monday 23 November 2026 as the closing time for both Fast Track (up to £500 for recording, releasing, equipment, training and networking) and Fast Track Live (£500 to £1,000 plus a career clinic, for tours, festivals, showcases, support slots and launch shows). Some third-party listings still show 20 November. Applicants can choose only one of the two programmes.

Why it matters: These are quick, practical grants for self-managing or self-releasing acts, and Fast Track Live is funded by the LIVE Trust specifically for grassroots live work. If you're developing an act with a confirmed live date in the next six months, this is a deadline to act on now, and it's worth checking the closing time on the official page.

Sources: Help Musicians: Fast Track Live · Help Musicians: Fast Track

Artist development

PRS Foundation opens Tempo Shift for women and gender-diverse creators

PRS Foundation has launched Tempo Shift, offering UK-based women and gender-diverse music creators grants of up to £5,000, up to £500 extra for childcare or caring costs, three mentoring sessions and a peer network. Applicants need more than 18 months' track record, and two places per round are kept for early-career creators. Applications close on 14 December 2026, and the first cohort will be announced in April 2027. Partners include TikTok and the PRS Members' Fund.

Why it matters: It adds a new funding route that combines money with mentoring, which suits artists building a longer-term career rather than funding a single release. Producers and behind-the-scenes creators can also put up to half of the grant towards equipment.

Sources: Record of the Day

Updates

Live music & touring

£1 arena ticket levy has raised £2.5m this year, says LIVE

Speaking in Sydney, LIVE chief executive Jon Collins said the voluntary £1 levy on tickets for shows above 5,000 capacity has raised £2.5 million so far this year. He said another £6 million is committed by participating artists, more than 130 tours now carry it, and the LIVE Trust expects to distribute £7 million to £8 million in 2027. New South Wales is weighing a similar scheme.

Why it matters: These are among the clearest figures yet on how much money is reaching grassroots live music, through schemes such as touring support and Fast Track Live. A bigger pot in 2027 could mean more funding rounds for small venues, promoters and developing acts to apply to.

Sources: WAtoday

Music marketing & social

UK under-16 social media ban could reach music streaming

According to Guardian reporting picked up by Music Ally, the regulations for the UK's under-16 social media ban could cover up to 150 platforms built around recommendation systems and user content, potentially including Spotify, despite earlier signals that music streaming would be exempt. The regulations are due before Parliament in December and are expected to take effect in spring 2027. The final list has not been published.

Why it matters: Under-16s are a core audience for many pop acts and family shows. If streaming or video platforms restrict younger users, campaigns aimed at teenagers may need to lean more on other channels, such as schools, parents and live events. Watch for the final regulations in December.

Sources: Music Ally · GOV.UK fact sheet

Entertainment law

Labels push back on Suno's request to pause second lawsuit

In a 13 November filing in Massachusetts federal court, Universal and Sony opposed Suno's request to pause their second lawsuit, which covers 60,202 recordings. They proposed that discovery continue on Suno's newer models, including v6, along with its training, licensing talks and finances, while questions about who owns each recording wait. If the case is paused, they asked for an order to preserve training data. Suno argues that rulings in the first case could narrow or resolve the second. The court has not yet decided.

Why it matters: The outcome will affect how quickly the courts examine whether 'licensed' AI music models still rely on earlier unlicensed training. That question matters for anyone weighing AI music tools or licensing catalogue to them.

Sources: AllHipHop · Music Business Worldwide (background)
